Retail investing has moved from a pandemic-era anomaly to a permanent structural feature of global capital markets. India's exchanges are adding a crore of new investors every few months, US options volume just hit a record 15 billion contracts, and the global IPO market raised nearly $172 billion in a single year. This roundup pulls together the most-cited, primary-sourced retail investing statistics for 2026 — covering market size, investor behavior, options and IPO activity, and where the data points next.

Key Retail Investing Statistics

The headline numbers from this report, each sourced from a named exchange, regulator, or research organization.

  1. NSE's unique registered investor base crossed 13 crore (130 million) as of April 27, 2026, up from 12 crore just seven months earlier. (Source: NSE, 2026)
  2. 62% of Americans reported owning stock in 2025, matching 2024 and tying the highest level in nearly two decades. (Source: Gallup, 2025)
  3. Retail investors made up 20.1% of US equity trading volume in Q3 2025, the second-highest share on record. (Source: Jefferies, 2025)
  4. Global options volume hit a record 15.2 billion contracts in 2025, up 24.4% from 2024. (Source: OCC, 2026)
  5. 1,293 IPOs raised $171.8 billion globally in 2025, a 39% jump in proceeds over 2024. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  6. India's IPO market raised $22.9 billion across a record 367 listings in 2025, up 9% in proceeds year on year. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  7. Only 8% of US investors began investing in the last two years as of the 2024 survey wave, down sharply from 21% in 2021. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  8. Individual investors held 18.7% of NSE-listed companies' market capitalization as of March 2026, directly and through mutual funds. (Source: NSE, 2026)

India's Retail Boom: Market Size and Growth

No market illustrates the scale of the retail shift better than India's, where NSE's investor base has compounded for five straight years.

NSE added its most recent crore of investors in just seven months — down from 14 years for the very first crore. — NSE, 2026
  1. NSE's unique registered investor base surpassed 13 crore (130 million) as of April 27, 2026, while total client accounts across brokers passed 26 crore by June 2026 (investors often hold codes with more than one broker). (Source: NSE, 2026)
  2. The investor base grew at a 26.4% CAGR from FY21 to FY26, well above the 15.2% CAGR of the preceding five-year period. (Source: NSE, 2026)
  3. It took 14 years for NSE to reach its first crore of investors; the most recent additions have come at roughly one crore every 6–8 months. (Source: NSE, 2026)
  4. NSE-listed company market capitalization reached ₹460.6 lakh crore by December 2025, an 18% CAGR. (Source: NSE, 2026)
  5. 7.2 crore new mutual-fund SIP accounts were opened in FY26 (April 2025–March 2026); average monthly SIP inflows rose from ₹3,660 crore in FY17 to ₹29,132 crore in FY26 — an eightfold increase in a decade. (Source: NSE, 2026)
  6. NSE investor participation now covers 99.85% of India's pin codes. (Source: NSE, 2026)
  7. Maharashtra leads India with more than 4 crore investor accounts (17% of the national total); the top 10 states together account for over 73%. (Source: NSE, 2025)
  8. NSE's Investor Awareness Programs increased fivefold to 17,764 in FY26, reaching 9.3 lakh participants; the Investor Protection Fund stood at ₹2,871 crore as of March 2026, up 16.8% year on year. (Source: NSE, 2026)

US & UK Participation: Who's Actually Investing

Stock ownership in the US sits at a two-decade high, but the gap between income and education groups remains wide — and across the Atlantic, most UK savers with meaningful assets are still sitting mostly in cash.

  1. 62% of Americans owned stock in 2025, up from a sub-60% average that persisted from 2010 to 2022. (Source: Gallup, 2025)
  2. Stock ownership reaches 87% among US households earning $100,000+, compared with just 28% among households earning under $50,000. (Source: Gallup, 2025)
  3. College graduates own stock at roughly twice the rate of adults with a high-school education or less (84% vs 42%). (Source: Gallup, 2025)
  4. Stock ownership varies sharply by race and ethnicity: 70% of White adults own stock, versus 53% of Black adults and 38% of Hispanic adults. (Source: Gallup, 2025)
  5. 61% of UK adults with more than £10,000 in investible assets held at least three-quarters of that money in cash rather than investments, as of the 2024 survey wave. (Source: FCA Financial Lives Survey, 2025)
  6. Only 8.6% of UK adults received financial advice on investments, pensions, or retirement planning in the 12 months to May 2024. (Source: FCA Financial Lives Survey, 2025)

Investor Behavior: Risk, Finfluencers, and Knowledge Gaps

FINRA's latest National Financial Capability Study — the most detailed periodic look at how US retail investors actually behave — shows a generation trading more aggressively while relying more heavily on social media.

Investors under 35 trade options at more than four times the rate of investors 55 and older. — FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025
  1. Only 8% of investors began investing within the last two years as of the 2024 survey, down sharply from 21% in the 2021 wave. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  2. Willingness to take substantial investment risk fell to 8% in 2024, from 12% in 2021; among investors under 35, it dropped to 15% from 24%. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  3. 34% of investors feel they need to take big risks to reach their financial goals, including 62% of investors under 35. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  4. Investors under 35 trade options at 43%, versus 10% for investors 55 and older; margin purchases follow the same pattern (22% vs 4%). (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  5. 29% of investors rely on social media as an investing information source; YouTube is used by 30% overall and 61% of investors under 35. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  6. 26% of investors use recommendations from social-media finfluencers when making investment decisions, rising to 61% among investors under 35. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  7. 13% of investors report buying meme stocks or other viral investments, rising to 29% among investors under 35. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  8. 27% of investors held cryptocurrency in 2024, unchanged from 2021, though only 26% were considering adding more exposure, down from 33% in 2021. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  9. The most-used sources of investment information are brokerage research and tools (75%), financial-professional recommendations (69%), business and finance articles (67%), and word of mouth (65%). (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)
  10. Investors answered an average of just 5.3 of 11 investing-knowledge quiz questions correctly; 75% of investors who actually trade on margin answered the margin question incorrectly. (Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation, 2025)

Options Trading: A Record-Setting Year

Options volume in the US didn't just grow in 2025 — it broke every prior annual record, with retail traders driving several single-day highs.

OCC cleared 15.2 billion total contracts in 2025 — a 24.3% increase over 2024, the largest annual jump in years. — The Options Clearing Corporation, 2026
  1. Total options volume reached 15.21 billion contracts in 2025, up from 12.22 billion in 2024 — a 24.4% increase. (Source: OCC, 2026)
  2. Equity options volume hit 8.27 billion contracts in 2025, up 26.8% year on year. (Source: OCC, 2026)
  3. ETF options volume reached 5.68 billion contracts in 2025, up 21.9% year on year. (Source: OCC, 2026)
  4. Index options volume reached 1.26 billion contracts in 2025, up 20.3% year on year. (Source: OCC, 2026)
  5. On October 10, 2025, OCC cleared 110 million option contracts in a single day — a new record, surpassing the 102.6 million set on April 4, 2025, driven overwhelmingly by retail buying during a volatile session. (Source: OCC data, 2025)
  6. Year-to-date average daily options volume reached 60.6 million contracts through December 2025, up 24.9% from 2024. (Source: OCC, 2026)

IPO Market: Back on Firmer Footing

After several sluggish years, 2025 marked a clear stabilization in global IPO activity — with India once again the world's busiest listing venue by deal count.

  1. 1,293 IPOs raised $171.8 billion globally in 2025, a 39% increase in proceeds over 2024 on a broadly unchanged deal count. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  2. Q4 2025 was the strongest quarter for global IPO volumes since Q4 2022 (381 deals) and the strongest for proceeds since Q4 2021 (US$60 billion). (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  3. India recorded a record 367 IPOs in 2025 (up 8% year on year), raising $22.9 billion (up 9%) — the world's most active listing destination by deal count. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  4. EMEIA led globally by deal count (42% of global IPOs), while Asia-Pacific led by proceeds (43% of global proceeds) in 2025. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  5. Private-equity-backed IPOs made up just 8% of deals (103 listings) but generated $62.1 billion — 36% of global IPO proceeds. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  6. US IPO proceeds hit $15.9 billion in Q3 2025 alone, nearly double the Q2 total; proceeds for the first nine months of 2025 reached $33 billion, up 21% year on year. (Source: EY, 2025)

Trading Tools & Platforms

Retail investors aren't just trading more — they're rethinking what a portfolio should even contain.

  1. Retail investors made up 20.1% of US equity trading volume in Q3 2025 — the second-highest share on record (behind only the early-2021 meme-stock peak) and nearly double the 15.8% long-term average since 2010. (Source: Jefferies, 2025)
  2. Two-thirds of Americans believe they need to look beyond stocks, bonds, and mutual funds for diversification, and more than 40% consider the classic 60/40 portfolio outdated. (Source: Charles Schwab Modern Wealth Survey, 2025)
  3. 62% of ETF investors say they could envision putting their entire portfolio into ETFs. (Source: Charles Schwab ETFs and Beyond Survey, 2025)

Future Outlook

Analysts see 2026 building directly on 2025's momentum, particularly around technology listings and India's continued role as a growth engine.

  1. EY expects 2026 could bring "one of the most significant cycles of technology listings in over a decade," with a potential wave of AI-driven IPOs on the horizon. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  2. Q4 2025 was already the strongest quarter for global IPO proceeds since Q4 2021, a momentum signal EY highlighted heading into 2026. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)
  3. India remained the world's most active IPO market by deal count in 2025, positioning it as one of EMEIA's key growth engines entering 2026. (Source: EY Global IPO Trends, 2026)

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of Americans own stock in 2026?

Based on Gallup's 2025 survey, 62% of Americans reported owning stock — directly or through mutual funds and retirement accounts — matching the 2024 level and the highest share in nearly two decades.

How many retail investors does India have?

NSE's unique registered investor base surpassed 13 crore (130 million) as of April 27, 2026, while total client accounts across brokers passed 26 crore by June 2026, according to NSE data.

What share of US stock trading comes from retail investors?

Retail investors made up 20.1% of US equity trading volume in the third quarter of 2025, according to Jefferies — the second-highest share on record, behind only the 2021 meme-stock peak.

Is options trading growing?

Yes. The Options Clearing Corporation cleared a record 15.2 billion options and futures contracts in 2025, up 24.3% from 2024, with growth led by equity and ETF options.

How big was the global IPO market in 2025?

1,293 companies went public worldwide in 2025, raising US$171.8 billion — a 39% jump in proceeds over 2024, according to EY's Global IPO Trends report.

Are younger investors taking on more risk than older investors?

Yes. FINRA Investor Education Foundation research found investors under 35 trade options at more than four times the rate of those 55 and older (43% vs 10%) and are far more likely to buy on margin or follow social-media finfluencers.
